# Stopped calls as solver seeds: the spike, its numbers and the chosen design

Moved out of `PARKED.md` (row "Stopped calls as solver seeds, and a trapped call's bounds guard as a solver query") on 2026-10-30; the row keeps a short summary and links here. The numbers are the row's text, unchanged except where marked.

## Decisions (2026-10-30, the user's "b1 c1")

- **Feature design: a queue of its own** (option (b) under Designs below). Not built.
- **Interface for the probe and the per-function miss survey: two hidden flags**: `--eval-only=NAME[,NAME…]` (also prints each call's outcome, solver and walk children included) and `--eval-miss`. This is one mechanism on the same path as `--eval-state`, and `tests/eval_sweep.sh` can drive it without env; it needs plumbing through `main.c`, `frontend.h`/`.c` and `check.h`. Sweep options today are hidden flags carried by `CheckOpts` (only `--eval-wrap` is in the usage text) and eval code has one env var, `CEREAL_LAZY_REPLAY`, marked "test only". Rejected: `--eval-only=` plus `CEREAL_EVAL_STATS=stops,miss` (two mechanisms, though it matches the `*_STATS` family) and env only, `CEREAL_EVAL_ONLY` and `CEREAL_EVAL_STATS` (about 10 lines, but a behavior-changing env var in eval code). The survey adds hundreds of lines per Lua run, so it is not printed always.
- Nothing is built. Building waits for the user's go. The memory diagnosis (every allocation of 64 MiB or more, with its function, in a trap sweep) is done, see Memory below; it decides the bound option (b) needs.
- Docs that go stale when it is built: `docs/EVAL_DESIGN.md` lines 592, 637 and 673, `docs/LAZY_BLOBS_DESIGN.md` line 149, `docs/LAZY_BLOBS_SOLVER_PLAN.md` lines 28, 63 and 118, and `PARKED.md` row "Pointer outcomes as solver choices under lazy blobs".

## Designs

(a) the spike as built (13 env knobs, the `Ev.gd` side channel, a guard query worth +11 to +34 taken for about 5300 queries): not recommended. (b) recommended: a call that traps, but not an unsupported one, goes into a bounded queue of its own (4 slots), is popped before `solve_step1` and walked by `flip_walk` (the loop `solve_step1` runs now, shared), with budget 4 + untaken/8 and at most 256, no guard query and no new evaluator semantics; it comes with the shadow cap and a bound on the memory a model can make the sweep allocate (open until the allocation behind the 1.3 to 2.7 GiB peaks is found: print every allocation of 64 MiB or more with its function in a trap sweep). Size, estimated from the spike's diff: about 145 new lines in `src/c/eval_run.c`, comments and blank lines included (306 added there, less the 66 lines of `flip_walk`, which is the loop `solve_step1` ran before, the guard query's 53 and about 40 lines of knobs, timing and counters), and about 17 for the shadow cap (`ev_shadow`, `EV_SHADOW_CAP` and the three places that allocate a shadow of an object's full size: `store_sh`, `ev_copy_n` in `eval.c` and `h_realloc` in `eval_libc.c`; the two sites for lazy objects, `lazy_byte` and `lazy_fill`, are bounded by `EV_LAZY_CAP`, 64 KiB, and stay as they are); the `Ev.gd` side channel (about 28 lines in `eval.c` and `eval_int.h`) goes. (c) in-bounds accesses as implicit steps of the path (`ev_access` would add `0 <= offset <= size - n` to the path, so solver children stay in bounds and the guard query goes): the largest change, not measured, kept here as the idea. A tagged `SSeed` in the corpus instead of a queue of its own is untested.

## The spike and its results

Measured on a throwaway spike, not built; which design to build is open.

### Mechanism

`search_keep` keeps only finishing calls as solver seeds, so a call that traps feeds the solver nothing. The spike keeps a call that traps in a queue of its own (4 slots, `CEREAL_SG`) and, before the normal solver steps, expands up to `CEREAL_GB` of them per function (plus one per `CEREAL_GADAPT` untaken edges of the function, at most 256; "walk runs" below count these expansions, each a tracked rerun), the walk's queries coming out of the function's usual 48. Two uses of a stopped call: the walk (`flip_walk`, the loop `solve_step1` already ran over a finishing seed's path, now also over the stopped call's own path: each branch before the stop whose other edge nobody has taken is flipped, solved and run; a child that traps again goes back into the queue) and the guard (a trap whose cause is a symbolic index becomes one query: the path prefix up to the access plus `0 <= base + scale*idx <= size - n` and `abs(idx) <= 2^31`; the access is recorded in `Ev.gd` by `ev_access` and the `TO_PTRADD` node). Knobs: `CEREAL_GWALK=1` walk, `CEREAL_GNOGUARD=1` skip the guard query, `CEREAL_GALL=1` keep any trap, `=2` any trap or unsupported call (default: out-of-bounds traps only), `CEREAL_GLAST=1` stopped seeds after the normal steps, `CEREAL_GOFF=1` ("spike off" below: the generator's stopped calls are not kept, but a solver child that stops out of bounds is still queued, so full Lua still shows 22 stopped seeds and 13 guard queries, `cpu-off-lua-s1234`), `CEREAL_SQ/SS/SE` normal budgets. Metrics: "taken" is the `solve:` line's `edges taken` (each function counted right after its own solve loop); "untaken" is the final `untaken edges` survey (callees and state variants too).

### Peak resident size

(`time` %M, the largest process of the tree): 1.26/1.67/2.71 GiB against 437/480/612 MiB for spike off (out-of-bounds only, seed 1234: 446 MiB). Sampled every 10 s from 17:52 to 18:41, 12 min into the sweeps (`rss_sample2.sh`; five sweeps: any trap on seeds 4321 and 777, spike off on 1234, 4321 and 777), z3 stays at or below 225 MiB, so cereal itself holds the rest, in steps (any trap, seed 4321: 218 MiB at 17:53:20, 1.41 GiB at 17:53:30). ### Memory

Five full-Lua sweeps on the first builds (`guard2`, `guard4`: no shadow cap) died with `cereal: fatal: out of memory` in the state pass after 33-35 min (`guard-lua-s1234`, `-s4321`, `-s777`, `guard-lua-gb64-s1234`, `walk-lua-s1234`; `guard-walk-lua-s1234` was stopped by hand); the same settings on the build with the cap finish (`cap-g-lua-s1234`, `cap-w-lua-s1234`, `cap-gw-lua-s1234`). Assumed cause, not isolated: the shadow arrays (4 bytes per byte of the object) of objects up to 1 GiB (the limit of `ev_new_obj`). The spike caps a shadow at 16 MiB (`EV_SHADOW_CAP`, `ev_shadow` in `src/c/eval.c`, used by `store_sh`, `ev_copy_n`, `h_realloc`: bytes stored into a bigger object stay concrete). No uncapped run with the spike off exists and the committed sweeps (`igoto-lua-*`, uncapped) finish, so whether the committed code can hit it is not known.

### Memory diagnosis (2026-10-30)

Run `2026-10-30-memprobe-trap-lua-s4321-r2` (`docs/runs.md`; the spike build `ab855aa` plus the patch plus a print in `ev_new_obj` and `ev_shadow` of every object or shadow of 64 MiB or more, with the swept function and the resident size, and a resident-size sample every 10 s; settings of `trap-lua-s4321`; 2146 s wall, 1746632 KB maxrss, the same 1.67 GiB as that run, 3322 taken, 958 queries). The raw prints, the samples, `instrument.py` and `memprobe_run.sh` are in the run folder.
- 158 prints, all objects, none a shadow (the 16 MiB shadow cap holds); 124 distinct sizes from 91 MB to 1,060,680,614 bytes, almost none a power of two: they are sizes the solver chose, below the 1 GiB limit of `ev_new_obj`.
- The one step: the sampler shows 223,608 to 1,479,196 KB at 11:11:21, within 10 s. The prints put it in `luaD_reallocstack__st2`, which allocated a 572,354,784-byte object while the resident size was 211,576 KB; the next print, in `luaF_newCclosure__st0`, reads 1,479,196 KB. The step, 1.26 GB, is about two arrays of that object (`b` and `init`, 2 x 572 MB = 1.14 GB) plus about 0.1 GB: the swept function's own code touched the whole object, which calloc alone would not do. The later 1 GB-size objects did not raise the resident size (it stays 1.48 to 1.52 GB; the highest print is 1.70 GB), so they were not touched.
- The touching code (read from the source, 2026-10-30): `luaD_reallocstack` (`ldo.c:239-240`) writes nil into every new stack slot, so a 572,354,784-byte object is 35,772,174 slots of 16 bytes, all written. Real Lua bounds `newsize` by `LUAI_MAXSTACK` (1,000,000 slots, 16 MB) only in a `lua_assert` (`ldo.c:224`), which the evaluator does not enforce, so the solver may pick any size. Not confirmed by stepping the evaluator.
- The committed code makes the same sizes but does not touch them (run `2026-10-30-k1-committed-lua-s4321-r2`; the committed tree `62f60c6` with the same print, no spike flags, seed 4321; 2153 s wall, 482544 KB maxrss): 157 prints, 153 objects (75 MB to 1,060,680,614 bytes) and 4 shadows of 3.9 to 7.2 GB; the 572,354,784-byte object in `luaD_reallocstack__st2` is among them. The largest resident size sampled was 281,980 KB, so those allocations stayed untouched (`calloc` pages) and nothing stepped. Shadows are not capped in the committed code (the spike's 16 MiB cap is why the spike printed none): they cost nothing until written. Inference: the step needs the spike's walk, which runs the call on past the allocation until the loop writes it; a trial of the committed code with a larger fuel would confirm it.
- Consequence for option (b): the bound needed is on the size of an object that code writes, not on how many are allocated. A cap on the bytes of one object that a call may create from a model value (for example 64 MiB, with a trap above it) would have stopped this step; it is the one number to measure before building. Real Lua tops out at 16 MB for this stack object, so a 64 MiB bound is safe for it; objects of 16 to 64 MiB were not printed (the print started at 64 MiB), so the largest legitimate object in the sweep is still unmeasured.

### Reading

The gain is "a call that traps is a seed", with no new evaluator semantics (the memory above needs a bound); the guard query is a poor increment; the earlier 2709 against 2739 result (row "Pointer structure of the sweep's generated state": trapped calls kept as seeds lowered coverage) was not re-run: the spike keeps stopped calls in a queue of their own, not among the corpus seeds, and there they raise coverage (+83 to +208 taken across the variants above; today's committed Lua baseline is 3087 to 3123 taken); a likely reason for the difference, untested, is that the finishing corpus keeps only 4 non-fresh seeds (`SSEEDS`), so stopped seeds there crowd finishing ones out.
